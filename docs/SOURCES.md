# Sources and Research Notes

Version 0.1 uses these sources to set guardrails, not to claim that its rubric has been empirically validated.

## Academic integrity and similarity

### APA Style — Avoiding Plagiarism Guide

[Avoiding Plagiarism Guide, APA Style 7th Edition](https://apastyle.apa.org/instructional-aids/avoiding-plagiarism.pdf)

Use: supports the distinction between genuine paraphrase and patchwriting, including the need to express sourced ideas in one's own wording and sentence structure while crediting the source.

### Turnitin — Similarity and flags

- [Interpreting the Similarity Score](https://help.turnitin.com/sv/integrity/studenter/lti/likhetsrapporten/interpreting-the-similarity-score.htm)
- [Flags in the Similarity Report](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/23351259442829-Flags-in-the-Similarity-Report)

Use: supports treating matching text and document flags as material for review rather than a plagiarism verdict. Turnitin explicitly notes that high similarity does not necessarily mean plagiarism and low similarity does not necessarily mean its absence.

## AI-text detection limits

### Weber-Wulff et al. — detector limitations

[Can we trust academic AI detective? Accuracy and limitations of AI-output detectors](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12331776/)

Use: reinforces caution about relying on automated detector outputs for academic judgments, particularly across different forms of assisted or transformed text.

### Russell, Karpinska, and Iyyer — human detection study

[People who frequently use ChatGPT for writing tasks are accurate and robust detectors of AI-generated text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15654)

Use: shows that experienced annotators in one controlled dataset used both lexical clues and broader qualities such as originality, clarity, and formality. It does not justify inferring authorship from a generic word blacklist or transferring its performance to arbitrary settings.

### Etaat — linguistic fingerprints

[Exploring linguistic fingerprints in human and AI-generated texts: An NLP-based approach in second language writing](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amper.2026.100258)

Use: supports the cautious premise that linguistic differences depend on the compared texts, tasks, and measures. Unsloop therefore treats features as review signals, not universal authorship markers.

## Research posture

Before changing Unsloop's claims or scoring model:

1. Prefer primary research and first-party guidance.
2. Record the population, task, model, language, and text transformation studied.
3. Separate findings about a controlled dataset from claims about general detection.
4. Note conflicts between studies rather than averaging them into false certainty.
5. Update the “last reviewed” date below.
