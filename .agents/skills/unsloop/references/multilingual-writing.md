# Multilingual and Cross-Language Writing

Read this file for translation, localization, bilingual drafting, cross-language source use, or voice matching across languages.

## Build a translation brief

Record source and target language or locale, intended audience, purpose, register, reading level, dialect preference, terminology authority, names and quotations policy, cultural or legal constraints, format, and whether the goal is literal translation, faithful translation, localization, transcreation, summary, or independent redrafting.

Do not assume language variety, identity, fluency, or cultural membership from prose.

## Preserve meaning and evidence

- Keep source meaning, translator choices, factual support, and target-language style distinct.
- Preserve uncertainty, qualification, attribution, and claim strength.
- Verify quotations against the correct language and version; identify whether a translation is official, supplied, independently translated, or back-translated.
- Keep cited source language and translated draft locations linked.
- Flag terms without reliable equivalents instead of inventing certainty.
- Do not use fluent phrasing to hide an unresolved source ambiguity.

## Handle voice

An author's style may not transfer feature-for-feature across languages. Build the target from supported higher-level traits such as directness, warmth, density, formality, cadence, and rhetorical posture. Distinguish the author's voice from translator conventions and target-genre norms. Report lower confidence when evidence is sparse or only cross-language samples exist.

## Review fairly

Do not label second-language features, code-switching, dialect, or culturally conventional structure as slop merely because they differ from a dominant-language norm. Evaluate clarity and suitability against the approved audience and purpose.

## Return the result

State the translation mode, source and target boundary, terminology decisions, unresolved ambiguities, quotation status, material adaptations, and confidence. In a translation Audit, leave both inspected source and target artifacts unchanged and present semantic corrections as separately authorized proposals. Recommend qualified linguistic, cultural, legal, or subject-matter review when the intended use makes it necessary.
