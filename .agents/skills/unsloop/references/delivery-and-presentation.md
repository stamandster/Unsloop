# Delivery and Presentation Writing

Read this file for speeches, lectures, lessons, presentations, narrated scripts, podcasts, video voiceovers, guided demonstrations, timed readings, or other writing whose real form includes live or recorded delivery, audience interaction, media, or synchronized output formats.

Treat delivery as part of the artifact, not as prose pasted onto a clock. Apply only the controls that affect the requested form.

## Establish the delivery contract

Extract or resolve:

- live, recorded, interactive, or hybrid setting;
- audience layers, prior knowledge, likely concerns, and intended final state;
- overall duration or length, whether it is a hard ceiling or working target, and any section allocations;
- intended pace and the basis for it rather than assuming one universal rate;
- quotations, readings, demonstrations, interviews, questions, pauses, media, and audience activity;
- delivery cues, accessibility or alternative-content needs, and platform constraints; and
- the authoritative source artifact, required derivatives, and validation expected for each format.

Ask about pace when it materially changes feasibility. If the user or governing domain supplies no rate, use a disclosed, conservative estimate only when the risk is low; otherwise request one. A pace estimate supports planning and never proves actual delivery time.

## Reconcile the whole and its parts

Confirm the overall constraint before drafting a tightly timed artifact. Add all section allocations and compare them with the whole. If the source, user request, and section total differ, show the discrepancy and obtain a decision before silently redistributing time or content.

Include every element that consumes delivery time or audience attention:

- spoken manuscript text and quoted or read material;
- pauses, reflection, questions, answers, and transitions;
- media setup, playback, observation, explanation, and follow-through;
- demonstrations, interviews, audience responses, and interaction; and
- openings, closings, accessibility descriptions, and operational handoffs.

Keep a proportionate safety buffer for natural variation, transitions, technical handling, or interaction. When the estimate exceeds the constraint, protect required material and the central outcome first; remove repetition or lower-value optional material before changing accepted allocations. Do not solve an overrun by assuming faster speech, omitted pauses, invisible setup time, or unexplained compression.

## Integrate evidence for an audience

When a quotation, passage, statistic, chart, demonstration, clip, or other evidence carries a material point, use this functional movement as needed:

1. **Need:** establish the question, claim, contrast, or decision the evidence addresses.
2. **Orient:** identify why this evidence is relevant and what the audience should notice, without giving away the full interpretation.
3. **Present:** show, read, play, or describe the material accurately within its source and modality boundary.
4. **Interpret:** explain what it supports, what it does not establish, and how it connects to the current point.
5. **Use:** identify the resulting understanding, feeling, decision, or action appropriate to the artifact.

This is a reasoning check, not a mandatory five-sentence formula. Use direct presentation when context already supplies the need and orientation. Do not drop evidence into the artifact as an unexplained authority cue, and do not manufacture application beyond what it supports.

## Make questions perform work

Use a material rhetorical or reflective question only when it has a clear function, such as opening inquiry, exposing contrast, guiding reasoning, preparing evidence, prompting self-examination, transitioning, or establishing the closing decision.

Check its placement, answer path, pause or response needs, and payoff. Do not stack decorative questions, ask what the audience cannot yet answer, answer so quickly that reflection is impossible, or leave a central question unresolved. A section may work better without a question; do not impose a quota.

## Design for a mixed audience

Do not treat a broad audience label as a complete model. Identify the shared entry point, concepts or terms that some people need explained, concerns that change reception, and worthwhile depth for more experienced audience members. Make the artifact accessible without flattening it or singling out a group unnecessarily.

## Govern optional media and interactions

For each optional item whose inclusion materially affects the artifact, give a compact decision brief:

- placement and governing requirement;
- what the audience will actually see, hear, or do;
- the claim, idea, task, or emotional movement it serves;
- what the audience should notice and the intended response;
- how it will be introduced, handled, interpreted, and followed through;
- time, space, accessibility, preparation, and technical cost; and
- the recommended disposition and a reasonable alternative when one exists.

Do not select consequential optional media silently. If the decision remains open and affects structure, timing, accessibility, evidence, or usability, keep the artifact **Provisional—decision required**. Required media remains required unless the governing authority changes it.

## Complete the closing movement

Make the ending produce the intended final reader or audience state. Reconnect the central purpose, resolve the governing question or tension, and provide an attainable next action when the genre calls for one. Do not merely recap, introduce a new major point, force a call to action, or continue after the artifact has achieved finality.

## Synchronize artifact formats

When one work exists in multiple formats, identify one authoritative source or an explicit bidirectional synchronization rule. After an accepted content change, regenerate or update every required derivative, then use the applicable artifact skill or tool to inspect rendering, pagination, media placement, speaker cues, accessibility, playback, or other format-specific behavior.

Record the version and validation actually checked. Mark derivatives stale or unresolved when they were not refreshed. Do not call formats synchronized, rendered, rehearsed, timed, playable, or accessible unless the corresponding comparison or validation occurred.

## Apply the mode contract

- **Write:** build or revise the artifact inside the confirmed delivery, evidence, media, timing, and format boundaries.
- **Review:** diagnose delivery logic, audience fit, evidence handling, questions, timing plausibility, media function, closing movement, and derivative consistency from what was actually inspected.
- **Audit:** preserve the inspected artifact and derivatives unchanged; compare them against the delivery contract, source evidence, timings, decisions, and format records, and keep every correction as a separate proposal.

At handoff, state only material delivery assumptions, measured or estimated basis, unresolved choices, included media, authoritative format, derivatives checked, and readiness limits. A polished manuscript is not proof of successful delivery or synchronized output.
