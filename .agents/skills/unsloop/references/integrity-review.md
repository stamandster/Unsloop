# Integrity Review

Read this file for source comparison, patchwriting analysis, plagiarism-risk review, evidence assessment, or a deep audit.

## Establish the evidence boundary

Record which materials are available. Limit claims accordingly.

Keep the writing brief distinct from the evidence corpus. A required claim in the brief is not supported until an appropriate source or other evidence supports it.

- With a draft only, flag unsupported claims and passages that warrant comparison; do not diagnose source dependence.
- With source excerpts, make findings only about the compared passages.
- With full sources, inspect context as well as local wording.
- With a similarity report, inspect the actual matches and exclusions; do not use the percentage as a verdict.

## Compare source relationships

Inspect five channels:

1. **Wording:** identical phrases, distinctive collocations, uncommon terminology, and metaphors
2. **Syntax:** retained sentence skeletons, clause order, and grammatical movement
3. **Idea order:** the same non-obvious conceptual sequence
4. **Detail selection:** the same incidental examples, qualifications, emphases, or omissions
5. **Rhetorical architecture:** the same setup, contrast, concession, illustration, and conclusion

Give necessary terminology and conventional organization less weight than distinctive choices.

## Classify the relationship

- **Proper quotation:** mark exact words as a quotation, cite them, and include a locator when appropriate.
- **Acceptable paraphrase:** credit the source and use independent wording and structure.
- **Too-close paraphrase:** retain distinctive wording or syntax despite crediting the source.
- **Structural dependence:** change surface wording while retaining unusual sequence, detail selection, or architecture.
- **Unattributed borrowing:** rely materially on source wording, ideas, or structure without adequate credit.
- **Secondary-source problem:** cite an original as if consulted when the wording or knowledge came through an intermediary.
- **Self-reuse:** reuse the writer's earlier language; assess disclosure and applicable policy separately.
- **Unsupported or fabricated support:** cite a missing source, misstate it, invent evidence, or attach it to a claim it does not support.

Do not use “plagiarism” as a catch-all. Describe the supported relationship and leave institutional intent or misconduct judgments to the responsible human process.

## Test the evidence

For each material claim, determine:

- what evidence supports it;
- whether the cited source makes the same claim;
- whether the draft overstates certainty;
- whether association became causation;
- whether the source is primary or secondary;
- whether attribution is close and clear;
- whether quotation wording and locators are accurate.

Flag strong terms such as “proves,” “causes,” “confirms,” “clearly,” and “undoubtedly” when the evidence does not warrant them.

## Correct source dependence

Choose the correction that fits the writer's purpose:

- quote distinctive language directly and attribute it;
- close the source, restate the idea from understanding, then compare for residual structure;
- synthesize multiple sources around the writer's own question or claim;
- reorganize the passage around the writer's reasoning rather than the source's paragraph;
- remove incidental details that were inherited but do no work;
- cite the intermediary honestly when the original was not consulted;
- narrow or qualify a claim to match the evidence.

Do not repair patchwriting by replacing words one at a time.
