# Website and Digital Content

Read this file when the artifact being written, reviewed, or audited is a website, web page, web application surface, content portal, or connected set of digital content. A website may instead be a research source; establish which role applies before proceeding. When it is both artifact and evidence corpus, keep those roles and findings separate.

## Select the content surface

Define the smallest scope that answers the request:

- **Single page:** one URL or supplied page artifact.
- **Page family:** a repeated type such as service, product, location, article, profile, help, or landing pages.
- **Journey:** the pages and interface copy a reader encounters while completing one goal.
- **Section or subdomain:** a bounded product, campaign, publication, documentation, support, or regional area.
- **Sampled site:** an explicitly selected sample used to identify likely patterns without claiming whole-site coverage.
- **Enumerated site:** a declared URL inventory or other bounded corpus whose inspected and inaccessible units are recorded.

Record the supplied, retrieved, rendered, and inaccessible material. Distinguish visible text, structured page data, metadata, navigation, link targets, images, transcripts, forms, interactive states, authenticated content, localized variants, and generated or personalized content. A homepage, sitemap, search result, crawl sample, screenshot, or successful HTTP response does not establish the condition of an entire site.

## Route by requested job

- **Review:** diagnose content effectiveness, structure, consistency, voice, evidence, reader movement, and page-to-page relationships. Preserve strong content and revise only when requested.
- **Write:** create or revise page content, content models, navigation labels, calls to action, metadata, help content, or migration mappings under an author-approved brief and source boundary.
- **Audit:** keep the inspected site or captured artifact unchanged. Report evidence-bound findings and proposed corrections separately. A live-site change requires a separately authorized implementation workflow.

For a live URL, use [source-safety.md](source-safety.md). For a scoped domain or broader research, use [source-acquisition.md](source-acquisition.md). For interconnected help, policy, or reference material, also use [documentation-systems.md](documentation-systems.md). For claimed reader outcomes, accessibility, or task success, use [usability-validation.md](usability-validation.md). When layout, rendering, implementation, analytics, security, legal compliance, or deployment matters, compose with the applicable specialist and keep its authority separate.

## Establish the website-content brief

Capture only what affects the work:

- organization, offering, topic, owner, and authoritative content source;
- site purpose and the specific reader outcomes or tasks;
- audience segments, prior knowledge, entry paths, objections, and accessibility or language needs;
- brand and voice evidence, required terminology, prohibited claims, and regulated or high-stakes boundaries;
- page or content type, lifecycle state, supported product, region, language, and version;
- conversion or action goal, its legitimate prerequisite information, and the decision owner;
- evidence-acquisition mode, analytics or search evidence actually available, and validation expected;
- inspection boundary, sampling rationale, inaccessible states, and stopping rule; and
- whether the result is findings, revised copy, a content plan, an inventory, migration mapping, or implementation-ready content.

Do not assume that conversion, search ranking, engagement, or brevity outranks accuracy, informed choice, accessibility, reader trust, or the user's stated goal.

## Cover the relevant use case

Apply the same evidence and voice method to the content family actually present. Common examples include:

- corporate, professional-service, nonprofit, community, religious, portfolio, and personal sites;
- home, about, service, product, feature, comparison, pricing, case-study, testimonial, contact, and FAQ pages;
- campaign and landing pages, lead-generation flows, newsletter signups, event pages, donation or membership appeals, and other conversion paths;
- ecommerce category, product, buying-guide, cart-support, shipping, return, warranty, and post-purchase content;
- SaaS and web-application onboarding, dashboards, settings, empty states, errors, confirmations, notifications, permissions, and interface microcopy;
- blogs, newsrooms, editorial publications, resource libraries, author pages, archives, and content hubs;
- documentation, developer portals, help centers, knowledge bases, tutorials, reference, troubleshooting, release, and migration content;
- educational, training, course, institutional, government, healthcare, financial, policy, and public-information pages, with qualified review where stakes require it;
- legal, privacy, consent, safety, and compliance-related content without treating Unsloop as legal or regulatory certification;
- multilingual, localized, regional, and accessibility-adapted sites;
- redesigns, rebrands, domain moves, CMS migrations, content consolidation, pruning, redirects, and post-launch maintenance; and
- internal portals, intranets, member areas, and authenticated content when access and privacy permit inspection.

This list illustrates content surfaces; it does not create separate modes or imply that every review must inspect every dimension.

## Review page and system layers

### Page purpose and reader movement

Test whether each page has a clear job, intended reader, entry context, needed information, sequence, next action, and relationship to adjacent pages. Headings and buttons should describe the real content or action. Calls to action should be proportionate, specific, and supported by the information a reader needs before acting.

### Message, evidence, and trust

Map material claims to support, qualification, source freshness, and page location. Check promises, comparisons, credentials, statistics, testimonials, case studies, prices, guarantees, availability, and implied outcomes for appropriate evidence and context. Do not invent social proof, urgency, scarcity, customer experience, performance, or legal assurance.

### Voice and cross-page consistency

Apply the authorized voice and Style Direction across navigation, headings, body copy, calls to action, forms, confirmations, errors, and support content without flattening legitimate content-type differences. Check terminology, naming, capitalization, tone, claim strength, audience assumptions, and duplicated explanations across pages.

### Findability and search presentation

Review information scent, navigation labels, internal links, page titles, descriptions, headings, summaries, and structured content when supplied or retrievable. Search intent and keywords may inform clarity and discoverability, but never justify keyword stuffing, misleading titles, unsupported claims, or prose written primarily for a ranking system. Distinguish content recommendations from verified technical SEO, indexation, ranking, traffic, or analytics conclusions.

### Accessibility and inclusive content

Review meaningful link text, heading sense, labels, instructions, error recovery, alternatives for non-text content, transcript or caption needs, reading load, and reliance on color, position, motion, sound, or imagery when evidence permits. Label model inspection or automated output accurately; do not claim conformance without the applicable standard, rendered implementation, tools, and qualified review.

### Lifecycle and maintenance

Identify canonical ownership, duplicates, dependencies, stale claims, broken or redirected destinations, supported versions, localization drift, orphaned content, publication state, review triggers, and retirement or redirect needs. A content rewrite is incomplete when changed promises, terminology, navigation, or product facts leave dependent pages inconsistent.

## Preserve measurement boundaries

Analytics, search-console data, experiments, recordings, surveys, support logs, conversion rates, and user tests are evidence only within their actual population, period, instrumentation, consent, and coverage. Absence of supplied data is not evidence that content succeeds or fails. A model may propose hypotheses and test plans, but it must not manufacture visitors, rankings, conversions, accessibility results, or observed behavior.

Keep these states distinct:

- **Content finding:** supported by inspected wording or structure.
- **Technical finding:** supported by an appropriate tool or implementation inspection.
- **Behavioral finding:** supported by actual analytics, research, or observed use.
- **Hypothesis:** a plausible explanation or experiment that remains unverified.

## Return a bounded result

Lead with the requested artifact or highest-consequence findings. State the audited scope, acquisition and capture method, inspected and inaccessible units, live or captured version, page or journey coverage, material strengths, findings, proposed changes, cross-page effects, validation actually performed, and unresolved gaps. For a sampled site, label conclusions as sample-bound. For live-site Audit, confirm that the site was not changed.
