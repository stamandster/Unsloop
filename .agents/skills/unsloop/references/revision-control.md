# Revision Control

Read this file for substantial revision, redline-style explanation, partial acceptance, conflicting versions, editorial rounds, or changes that could alter meaning, evidence, voice, requirements, or approval state.

## Establish the revision contract

Record the authoritative input version, requested outcome, allowed scope, protected material, governing requirements, evidence boundary, desired comparison form, and approval cadence. Protected material may include author-supplied observations, questions, interpretations, or tentative perspectives. Do not broaden “copyedit” into substantive rewriting or treat “make it better” as permission to change the writer's position.

When revision follows Audit, preserve the audit as a separate non-mutating record. Record whether authorization permits proposals only, presentation-only edits, specified meaning-changing edits, or an explicitly bounded audit-and-revise batch. Audit findings do not authorize their own application.

## Classify changes

Use stable `CHG-*` records when a project needs persistent control. Classify each material change as:

- **Correction:** fixes an objective error or broken requirement;
- **Clarification:** makes existing meaning easier to understand;
- **Integrity repair:** corrects support, attribution, quotation, or source dependence;
- **Voice restoration:** returns prose to the evidenced voice target;
- **Structural revision:** changes order, emphasis, or argument movement;
- **Substantive change:** changes a claim, position, recommendation, scope, or conclusion; or
- **Copyedit:** fixes surface correctness without intended meaning change.

Use dispositions **Proposed**, **Accepted**, **Partially accepted**, **Rejected**, **Revision requested**, **Applied**, or **Superseded**.

## Preserve personal perspective without laundering facts

Before removing material for lack of external verification, classify what it is:

- **Externally checkable factual claim:** requires support, qualification, correction, or an explicit unresolved status appropriate to the genre.
- **Personal observation or experience:** may establish what the author reports noticing or experiencing, not what is universally true.
- **Interpretation or tentative perspective:** expresses the author's reading, judgment, or provisional conclusion and must not be upgraded to verified fact.
- **Unresolved question:** identifies an inquiry or uncertainty rather than asserting an answer.

When personal material is relevant and useful, preserve it with the smallest genre-appropriate boundary. Scope it in prose, a heading, a callout, a note, or project metadata—for example, “In my experience,” “My reading is,” or “An open question is”—without forcing a visible tag into writing where natural framing is already clear. Label the basis as author-supplied, personal observation, interpretation, tentative, or unresolved when evidence status could otherwise be mistaken.

Separate any embedded general claim from the personal perspective and verify or qualify that claim independently. A perspective label does not make a factual assertion supported. Do not invent a first-person experience, strengthen a tentative view, preserve material the user authorized for removal, or retain content that conflicts with a governing safety, legal, privacy, or publication requirement. Lack of external verification by itself is not a reason to delete an otherwise useful author-supplied perspective.

## Protect consequential revisions

Before a consequential or bulk revision:

1. resolve the exact files and ranges;
2. identify downstream effects on claims, citations, quotations, requirements, terminology, links, summaries, conclusions, and dependent artifacts;
3. preserve a user-authorized version-control checkpoint or affected-file snapshot with hashes;
4. preview the change plan;
5. obtain approval when the revision changes an author-owned position, locked requirement, approved wording, evidence conclusion, or external commitment;
6. apply changes in bounded batches; and
7. reconcile the manuscript, ledgers, and resume state.

When the work has required derivatives, include them in the impact map. Refresh and validate them after accepted changes or mark them stale; do not let a revised canonical source coexist with apparently final but outdated outputs.

Never overwrite an existing checkpoint or unrelated user work.

Before applying an audit-derived revision, compare the before and after versions for claim, position, recommendation, conclusion, scope, certainty, evidence strength, chronology, quantity and unit, attribution, causality, condition, exception, and exclusion. Treat any difference in those fields as meaning-changing even when the prose sounds clearer.

## Process disposition

- **Accepted:** apply and record the accepted scope.
- **Partially accepted:** apply only the identified passages, decisions, or effects.
- **Rejected:** keep the change out of the active artifact and future revisions.
- **Revision requested:** revise the proposal without marking it Applied.
- **Superseded:** retain the history when it explains the current artifact.

Silence is not acceptance when a checkpoint was requested. Rejected suggestions must not leak into later drafts.

## Return revisions

Return the requested artifact first. When change explanation is useful, provide a compact record with location, before/after summary, classification, reason, evidence or requirement effect, voice effect, personal-perspective scope when material, and disposition. Preserve strong untouched material and avoid narrating trivial mechanical edits unless requested.
