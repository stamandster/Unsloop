## Select mode and review type

Use **Review** for constructive diagnosis based mainly on the manuscript. Use **Audit** when the task requires evidence-heavy comparison against canon, chronology, project records, research, source works, historical facts, or explicit requirements.

Select only the contracts that fit the request and project stage:

- **Developmental:** premise, narrative promise, intended experience, structure, character movement, and ending alignment.
- **Structure and pacing:** causality, escalation, reversals, quiet space, chapter function, subplot integration, and scene order.
- **Character and relationships:** motivation, agency, capability, limitation, change, emotional continuity, and relationship dynamics.
- **Continuity and chronology:** canon, names, objects, injuries, locations, travel, ages, timing, knowledge, and causal dependencies.
- **POV and narration:** access, person, tense, narrative distance, reliability, head-hopping, switching rules, and viewpoint discipline.
- **Dialogue:** character distinction, objective, subtext, relationship, exposition, rhythm, and consistency with knowledge and setting.
- **Theme, motif, setup, and payoff:** recurrence, implication, overstatement, forgotten setups, premature reveals, and earned payoff.
- **Line edit:** clarity, rhythm, imagery, precision, redundancy, cliché, abstraction, formulaicity, and voice preservation.
- **Copyedit:** grammar, spelling, punctuation, capitalization, typography, internal style, and terminology after structural stability.
- **Simulated reader response:** likely attention, confusion, expectation, tension, and emotional response for the stated audience.
- **Research and historical accuracy:** real-world factual support, anachronism, access boundary, uncertainty, and story consequences.
- **Adaptation and source dependence:** wording, syntax, idea order, distinctive details, characters, worlds, and rhetorical or plot architecture.
- **Authenticity and representation questions:** textual risks, assumptions, missing context, possible stereotypes, and questions for qualified lived-experience review.

For “review my novel” without a focus, begin with a standard developmental review and the most consequential continuity or voice issues visible in the inspected boundary. Do not run every contract at maximum depth.

## Establish the review boundary

Record:

- manuscript version, files, chapters, scenes, or excerpts inspected;
- project records and research inspected or unavailable;
- project stage and intended reader experience;
- requested review type and depth;
- relevant author decisions and locked constraints;
- whether rewriting, examples, or diagnosis only was requested; and
- any context limit that prevents manuscript-wide conclusions.

When reviewing an excerpt, distinguish local findings from hypotheses requiring the full manuscript. When project records conflict with accepted prose, identify the conflict rather than choosing silently.

## Apply review contracts

For each selected contract, test observable relationships rather than generic ideals.

### Developmental

Ask whether the premise generates sustained pressure, scenes fulfill the narrative promise, choices cause meaningful consequences, arcs change rather than reset, subplots alter the main movement, and the ending resolves or deliberately transforms the established expectations.

### Structure and pacing

Map scene function and state change. Distinguish intentional quiet from stalled movement, useful delay from repetition, and surprise from missing causality. Identify the smallest structural intervention before recommending wholesale reordering.

### Character and relationship

Track goals, pressures, choices, knowledge, capabilities, emotional state, and relationship change across the inspected range. Do not demand likability, explicit self-awareness, or a conventional positive arc.

### Continuity and chronology

Compare accepted manuscript, canon, characters, timeline, scene state, and knowledge. Report exact locations, contradiction type, affected downstream material, confidence, and proposed resolution. Treat uninspected ranges as unknown.

### POV and narration

Identify the active narrative contract, then flag access or distance changes only when they violate it or create unintended confusion. Preserve deliberate omniscience, free indirect style, unreliability, or controlled switching.

### Dialogue

Evaluate whether speech reflects character, relationship, objective, knowledge, setting, and immediate pressure. Do not create distinction through arbitrary accents, errors, catchphrases, or phonetic caricature.

For multiple recurring speakers, compare each passage to the applicable Confirmed `CVP-*` profile. Test attribution without tags, stable personality, baseline tone, cadence, syntax, diction, discourse habits, knowledge, relationship posture, contextual variation, cast contrast, and restraint. Report silent drift separately from purposeful evolution. Do not revise a profile to excuse inconsistent prose; only an explicit author disposition can approve a new version.

### Theme, motif, setup, and payoff

Track recurrence and function. Distinguish deliberate motif from accidental repetition, implication from lecture, and fair setup from conspicuous signaling.

### Line and copy

Preserve narrative and character voice. Do not line-edit unstable structure as though it were final, and do not treat a house-style preference as an objective error.

### Research, adaptation, and integrity

Use [integrity-review.md](integrity-review.md) and [source-verification.md](source-verification.md). Keep story canon separate from real-world support and describe publication or licensing questions as unresolved policy matters, not legal verdicts.

## Prioritize findings

Each material finding should include:

- manuscript location;
- observed feature;
- supported interpretation or unresolved question;
- consequence for the selected review contract and intended experience;
- evidence basis and confidence;
- downstream scenes or project records affected;
- strong material to preserve; and
- the smallest useful intervention.

Rank manuscript-level causes before repeated symptoms. Group recurring instances instead of producing an exhaustive error dump unless the user requests a copyedit inventory.

## Protect reader and authenticity claims

Label simulated reader response as hypotheses based on the stated audience and inspected text. It is not real beta-reader evidence. Do not present it as beta-reader data, market research, or proof of how real readers will respond.

For authenticity and representation questions:

- identify concrete textual choices and plausible risks;
- separate researchable facts from lived-experience judgment;
- do not claim to speak for a culture, identity, profession, disability, trauma, or community;
- recommend qualified sensitivity, authenticity, or subject-matter review when material; and
- do not infer author intent from the depicted material.

## Return the result

Return:

1. **Overall assessment:** selected review type, manuscript boundary, and highest-level judgment.
2. **Priority findings:** location, observation, consequence, evidence, confidence, downstream impact, and smallest intervention.
3. **Continuity or requirement map:** only for evidence-heavy or multi-part work.
4. **What should remain unchanged:** effective structure, scenes, voice, character work, or deliberate irregularity.
5. **Revision sequence:** only when multiple changes depend on one another.
6. **Revised example or manuscript:** only when requested.
7. **Evidence and readiness limits:** uninspected ranges, missing records, research, authority, or professional review.

Do not bury the assessment under a checklist or represent a focused review as a complete manuscript evaluation.
