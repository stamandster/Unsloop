# Source Verification

Read this file when the user asks to verify citations, quotations, claims, page numbers, or source relationships.

Maintain a compact verification record: claim or quotation, cited source and version, material inspected, access level, result, and unresolved limitation.

## Verify in order

1. Identify the exact edition, version, publication, or page the draft cites.
2. Prefer the original source over a search snippet, aggregator, or secondary discussion.
3. Inspect enough surrounding context to understand the source's actual claim and limitations.
4. Check quotation wording, omissions, alterations, and locator.
5. Compare the draft's strength and scope with the source.
6. Record whether the check used full text, an abstract, a snippet, metadata, or an intermediary.

## Distinguish source status

- **Verified:** inspect the relevant original passage or authoritative record.
- **Partially verified:** confirm metadata or an abstract but not the relevant full passage.
- **Secondary confirmation:** rely on a credible intermediary that describes the original.
- **Unverified:** cannot access enough evidence to confirm the claim.

Do not turn partial or secondary confirmation into “verified.”

## Check for citation laundering

Look for a chain in which the draft cites Source A but appears to have learned the language or claim through Source B or C. Require the writer to cite the source actually consulted and use secondary-citation conventions when the original is unavailable.

## Report conflicts

If sources disagree, state the disagreement and the basis of each claim. Do not silently choose the result that best supports the draft.

## Protect the record

Use short excerpts needed for comparison. Do not reproduce long protected passages. Preserve exact links or bibliographic details for sources actually checked.

Do not imply that checking one passage validates the whole source, every claim attached to it, or the completeness of the writer's source corpus.
