## Establish the handoff boundary

Confirm:

- authoritative manuscript version and accepted units;
- intended audience, genre, approximate length, and positioning;
- requested artifact and recipient or channel;
- governing submission instructions, template, or word limit;
- manuscript ranges and project records inspected;
- unresolved placeholders, research, permissions, or decisions; and
- whether document conversion or formatting is in scope.

Do not treat an assembled file as proof that the story is creatively or editorially complete.

## Distinguish readiness stages

Use these stages descriptively, not as a universal certification:

- **Creatively complete:** the intended story has an ending and no known missing story unit.
- **Structurally revised:** major premise, arc, causality, pacing, and continuity decisions are resolved within the inspected boundary.
- **Line-edited:** sentence-level clarity, rhythm, specificity, and voice have received a deliberate pass.
- **Copyedited:** grammar, spelling, punctuation, terminology, and internal style have received a deliberate pass.
- **Assembled:** accepted units are ordered into the requested manuscript output and structural gaps are reported.
- **Submission package prepared:** requested synopsis, query, pitch, blurb, or checklist follows the supplied requirements within the evidence boundary.

Do not use one stage to imply another. State when a stage is user-reported rather than inspected.

## Assemble the manuscript

Before assembly:

1. identify the accepted manuscript units and intended order;
2. exclude Planned, Drafted, Revised, Cut, Archived, branch, and rejected units unless explicitly selected;
3. check duplicate IDs, missing units, ordering gaps, unresolved placeholders, and contradictory status;
4. create or confirm a recoverable checkpoint when assembly changes files;
5. preview the source list and destination;
6. refuse to overwrite an existing destination; and
7. assemble deterministically with an output manifest.

Use the optional project script when available for Markdown units. When it is unavailable or the manuscript format differs, follow the same contract with host-native document tools. Hand off DOCX, PDF, EPUB, or other format work to the appropriate artifact workflow rather than inventing unsupported conversion.

## Prepare supporting artifacts

- **Synopsis:** reveal the full causal story, major turns, character movement, and ending at the requested length. Do not write it like jacket copy unless requested.
- **Query letter:** follow supplied agent or publisher requirements; state supportable title, genre, length, premise, comparison context supplied by the user, and relevant biography only from verified user material.
- **Blurb:** create reader-facing promise and stakes without fabricating endorsements, awards, reception, or manuscript events.
- **Pitch or logline:** compress the protagonist or focal pressure, differentiating situation, central conflict, and stakes without replacing specificity with hype.
- **Character or series summary:** distinguish series-wide canon from book-specific arcs and avoid revealing more than the recipient needs.
- **Submission checklist:** map every supplied requirement to the artifact, status, and unresolved action.

Marketing language must remain supportable by the manuscript and user-provided facts. Do not invent credentials, sales, representation, awards, comparisons, endorsements, or market evidence.

## Apply publication boundaries

Unsloop may prepare and audit materials, but it does not certify legal clearance, marketability, commercial viability, cultural authenticity, professional copyediting, agent interest, publisher acceptance, or publication readiness beyond the inspected requirements.

Flag licensing, permissions, real-person depiction, adaptation, quotation, defamation, confidentiality, or platform-policy questions for qualified review when material. Do not give a legal verdict.

## Return the handoff

Return the requested manuscript or supporting artifact first, then a concise handoff containing:

- source manuscript version and inspected boundary;
- readiness stages established, user-reported, or unresolved;
- included and excluded manuscript units;
- assembly or requirement warnings;
- facts or claims requiring confirmation;
- next practical action; and
- an honest readiness label only when it helps the user decide whether to use the artifact.
