# Character Voice Continuity

Read this file whenever fiction contains multiple speaking characters, a recurring character crosses scenes or sessions, the user wants to establish a character's personality or speech, or dialogue voice is being reviewed or changed.

## Establish each character

Use the user's explicit character decisions first. At the beginning of a sustained project, let the user define any combination of personality, worldview, temperament, default tone, education, era, background, relationship posture, vocabulary, cadence, syntax, verbal habits, emotional tells, and avoided language.

When material elements remain open, offer two or three materially different voice proposals based on story function, goals, history, setting, relationships, knowledge, current pressures, and the approved world. Explain how each option would sound and what contrast it creates with the existing cast. Mark every model-suggested trait **Proposed** until the user accepts it.

Do not derive personality or speech mechanically from race, nationality, gender, disability, class, age, religion, dialect, or another identity category. Context may inform a researched language environment; it does not justify caricature or demographic shorthand.

For a short one-off scene, keep compact in-context character voice cards. For persistent multi-character work, use `CHARACTERS.md` plus `CHARACTER-VOICES.md`.

## Build a voice profile

Create one versioned `CVP-*` profile for each recurring speaking character. Keep these fields distinct:

- stable core personality, values, contradictions, wants, defenses, and social posture;
- default tone and level of directness, warmth, humor, restraint, certainty, or formality;
- cadence, sentence length, syntax, interruption, questions, fragments, and silence;
- diction, idiom, metaphor domains, technical vocabulary, taboo or avoided expressions;
- discourse habits such as explaining, deflecting, challenging, qualifying, storytelling, or asking;
- distinctive but non-gimmicky markers;
- knowledge, misconceptions, and subjects the character cannot name or understand yet;
- relationship-specific shifts;
- permitted variation under stress, intimacy, fear, authority, fatigue, deception, or growth;
- effective manuscript range; and
- basis and approval decision.

Make characters distinguishable through selection, reasoning, rhythm, and social behavior—not catchphrase repetition or exaggerated accent spelling. Similar backgrounds may produce overlap; distinction need not mean maximal contrast.

## Lock the accepted profile

Use **Proposed**, **Confirmed**, or **Superseded** profile state.

A Confirmed profile is immutable for drafting purposes until the user explicitly approves an override or evolution after review. Do not silently normalize, enrich, simplify, merge, or drift it. New autonomous details remain Proposed.

Before each recurring-character scene, load the relevant Confirmed profile, relationship state, knowledge state, effective range, and immediate emotional context. After drafting, compare the dialogue with those records. A polished line that violates the locked profile is still a continuity issue unless the manuscript provides a deliberate cause.

## Draft contextual variation

Consistency does not mean identical speech in every situation. Vary expression only within the profile's permitted range and the current story state:

- who the character is addressing;
- what the character wants or conceals;
- power and intimacy in the relationship;
- knowledge available at that moment;
- physical and emotional state;
- public versus private setting;
- deliberate performance, code-switching, deception, or masking; and
- accepted character development.

Preserve the stable core beneath the variation. If the needed line falls outside the approved range, propose the change rather than inventing a justification after the fact.

## Review distinction and drift

For dialogue review, test:

1. **Attribution test:** without dialogue tags, can the likely speaker be inferred for defensible reasons rather than a gimmick?
2. **Profile test:** do tone, syntax, diction, habits, knowledge, and relationship posture fit the effective Confirmed profile?
3. **Context test:** does variation follow the immediate objective and emotional state?
4. **Cast contrast test:** have multiple characters converged on the same explanatory rhythm, wit, vocabulary, or rhetorical competence?
5. **Drift test:** did a tendency change gradually with accepted development, or merely because the drafting model changed?
6. **Restraint test:** are markers selective enough to remain human rather than mechanical?

Report the location, current locked trait, observed deviation, possible contextual explanation, consequence, confidence, and smallest useful correction. Do not rewrite all dialogue into sharper contrast when the existing overlap is plausible.

## Override or evolve a profile

When the user requests or accepts a voice change:

1. show the current Confirmed profile and the exact proposed differences;
2. explain why the change is useful and provide a short neutral example when helpful;
3. ask the user to **Keep**, **Approve evolution**, **Approve retroactive override**, or **Request revision**;
4. identify affected scenes, relationships, knowledge, arcs, and other character contrasts;
5. create a recoverable checkpoint before changing accepted prose or persistent state;
6. create a new Proposed profile version;
7. after explicit acceptance, mark it Confirmed and the prior version Superseded;
8. record the decision and effective scene or chapter range; and
9. revise only the approved scope, then recheck dialogue distinction and continuity.

An **evolution** begins at an approved story point and preserves earlier dialogue. A **retroactive override** changes the character design for prior and future prose and therefore requires an impact map. Rejection leaves the existing Confirmed profile unchanged, and rejected traits must not leak into later drafting.

## Protect evidence and fairness

Treat accepted manuscript dialogue and author decisions as story evidence, not evidence about real people or demographic groups. Do not import personal facts from the user's voice samples into a character. Do not mimic an identifiable real person's distinctive speech without authorization. Convert named-author or performer requests into broad non-exclusive traits.
