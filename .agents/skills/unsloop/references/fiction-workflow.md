# Fiction Workflow

Read this file for every fiction request in Write, Review, or Audit: an isolated scene, flash fiction, short story, novella, novel, serial, shared world, or multi-book series. Fiction is a specialization of the existing modes, not a fourth mode.

When more than one speaking character appears, a recurring character crosses units, or the user asks to establish or change a character's personality or speech, also read [character-voice-continuity.md](character-voice-continuity.md).

Treat every subject and genre as available unless a governing rule or the user's boundary excludes it. Do not assume a religious, biblical, literary, commercial, or other subject context. Use **story canon** for facts established inside the fiction; do not call the project record a “story bible.”

## Select the fiction job

- Choose **Write** for discovery, brainstorming, planning, drafting, author-requested revision, continuation, assembly, synopsis, query, blurb, or publication-support writing.
- Choose **Review** for broad critique, developmental editing, structure, pacing, character, relationship, POV, narration, dialogue, theme, prose, line editing, copyediting, simulated reader response, or authenticity questions.
- Choose **Audit** for evidence-heavy continuity, chronology, canon, research, historical accuracy, adaptation, attribution, source-dependence, or requirement comparison.

For a broad request about an existing manuscript, default to Review. For a broad request about an idea or premise, use Write and begin discovery. When the task combines modes, identify the primary deliverable and apply the other lens only where it materially helps.

Read [fiction-project-operations.md](fiction-project-operations.md), [fiction-review.md](fiction-review.md), or [fiction-publication.md](fiction-publication.md) when the selected job needs those procedures.

## Scale the workflow

| Request scale | Default handling | Persistent state |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated scene, flash fiction, or exploratory passage | Build only the brief needed for the requested unit. | None unless requested. |
| Short story or continuing multi-scene work | Establish a compact contract and scene sequence. | Propose compact state only when cross-session continuity becomes useful. |
| Novella, novel, or serial | Establish the creative contract, architecture, scene state, and resumable checkpoint before sustained drafting. | Propose full state once and create it only after approval. |
| Series or shared world | Separate series-wide canon from book-specific arcs, state, and manuscripts. | Use series state after approval. |

Do not impose project files, cadence selection, or novel architecture on a clear one-off scene. Inspect existing manuscripts and project records before proposing a layout, and never silently reorganize them.

## Establish the fiction brief

Apply the topic path in [writing-brief.md](writing-brief.md). A fiction topic may be a premise, image, conflict, character, setting, genre idea, story question, or existing manuscript.

For brainstorming, offer genuinely different narrative engines, central pressures, intended reader experiences, forms, genre fits, and research burdens—not superficial variants of one plot.

Extract before asking. Capture only fields that affect the current stage:

1. form, scale, and starting state;
2. premise and narrative promise;
3. intended reader experience;
4. audience, positioning, genre, and tonal range;
5. POV system, person, tense, distance, reliability, and switching rules;
6. author voice evidence, narrative design, viewpoint voice, and dialogue distinctions;
7. characters, relationships, setting, conflict, chronology, arcs, themes or questions, known scenes, and ending direction;
8. real-world research boundary and invented rules;
9. content, format, length, deadline, continuity, and series constraints;
10. locked author decisions and decision authority when collaborators are involved; and
11. collaboration cadence and maximum batch for sustained creation; and
12. recurring-character personality and speech decisions: user-defined, model-proposed for approval, or a deliberate mixture.

Do not require a moral, lesson, market category, or predetermined structure. For sustained work, summarize a compact creative contract and mark consequential elements Known, Inferred, or Unknown before architecture or drafting.

## Choose collaboration cadence

For sustained creation, determine cadence when it is not already clear:

- **Guided:** approve every major development phase and each drafting unit.
- **Adaptive (Recommended):** approve the creative contract, architecture, and drafting batches; pause again for consequential deviations or unresolved choices.
- **Autonomous:** approve the creative contract and maximum batch, then work through that checkpoint.

Use a structured control when available, with `Cadence` as the header and the three choices above; otherwise ask the same choice conversationally. Do not change the host's collaboration or execution mode merely to obtain the control.

Cadence controls pause frequency, not story ownership. In every cadence, preserve locked decisions. In Autonomous work, keep unaccepted details Proposed, stop at the approved batch, and pause before changing Confirmed canon, premise, ending direction, POV system, content boundaries, real-person treatment, or intended reader experience.

## Develop and draft

Move through only the phases the work needs:

1. **Discover:** topic, seed, premise, and intended experience.
2. **Contract:** scope, narration, voice, boundaries, research, cadence, and locked decisions.
3. **Foundation:** characters, relationships, setting, world rules, chronology, and central pressure.
4. **Architecture:** plot spine, arcs, subplots, reveals, setups, payoffs, escalation, and ending direction.
5. **Scene design:** causal sequence, scene purpose, POV, objective or pressure, turn, consequence, knowledge, continuity, and research.
6. **Draft:** write the approved unit or batch, then check POV, tense, distance, knowledge, motivation, spatial and temporal continuity, dialogue distinction, causality, voice, and research.
7. **Accept:** present the prose first, then material decisions and Proposed details; update state only according to the user's disposition.
8. **Revise:** separate contract, structure, character, continuity, POV, theme, research, prose, line, and copy passes when combining them would obscure causes.

Support both discovery writing and outline-driven writing. Do not force three-act structure, the hero's journey, identical scene beats, artificial conflict, or exhaustive outlining.

For persistent work, use [fiction-project-operations.md](fiction-project-operations.md) to maintain state, acceptance, branches, impact maps, and recovery. Establish a versioned voice profile for every recurring speaking character. User-defined traits take precedence; model suggestions remain Proposed until accepted.

## Review or audit

Use [fiction-review.md](fiction-review.md) to select a proportionate review contract. Return the assessment and highest-value findings before optional rewrites. Distinguish critique from evidence-heavy comparison, and never claim that a simulated response represents real readers or a community.

## Complete and package

Use [fiction-publication.md](fiction-publication.md) when assembling manuscript files or preparing completion and submission artifacts. Distinguish creative completion, structural revision, line editing, copyediting, assembly, and submission-package readiness.

## Protect voice and integrity

Keep these channels separate:

- authorized evidence of the user's writing tendencies;
- the designed narrative voice;
- viewpoint-character perception and language;
- character-specific dialogue; and
- real-world research evidence.

Do not import facts, autobiography, characters, settings, opinions, or memorable wording from voice samples. Convert named-author imitation requests into broad, non-exclusive traits without copying signature language, protected characters, distinctive worlds, or recognizable rhetorical sequences.

Story canon establishes what is true inside the fiction, not what is verified in the real world. Fiction may invent people, events, dialogue, worlds, and emotions when clearly presented as fiction. Do not present invention as authentic quotation, personal testimony, allegation, or factual verification. Never claim originality against an incomplete comparison corpus.

Confirmed character voice profiles are locked author decisions. Draft emotional, relational, and situational variation within them, but do not silently drift, flatten the cast into one voice, or change a profile. Apply an author-approved evolution or retroactive override through the versioned change process in [character-voice-continuity.md](character-voice-continuity.md).

## Handle limits

- When existing files disagree, state what was inspected and resolve authority before mutation.
- When context is limited, load the resume packet, relevant records, and necessary manuscript range; bound every global claim accordingly.
- When research is unavailable, mark it unresolved or draft around it without inventing verification.
- When project files are declined, work conversationally and offer a compact resume packet.
- When the project changes premise, genre, POV, ending, or another locked decision, treat it as a consequential change.
- When a draft discovers a better direction, preserve it as a proposal until accepted.
- When a small request grows, propose persistent state only when it becomes materially useful.

Return fiction before process notes. Never represent fluent prose, complete ledgers, or a reached word count as proof that the work is finished.
